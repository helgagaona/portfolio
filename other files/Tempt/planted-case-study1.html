UX/UI case study · Personal project

Helping new plant owners decide when to act and when to wait

Planted is a context-aware plant-care feature that uses recent care history to give beginners one clear next step.

Read the case study ↓
RoleUX/UI designer · Solo
PlatformMobile feature
ToolsFigma · FigJam
ScopeOne focused feature

01 · The problem

Plant-care advice often starts with an action

Most plant-care apps give users immediate instructions based on a symptom without considering what they have already tried. Beginners may keep changing the water, soil, light, or location, creating more stress instead of solving the problem.

Design goal

Help users pause before they overcorrect

Planted records recent care history and helps beginners decide whether to make one change, continue observing, or wait during the plant's adaptation period.

02 · Research

Context changes the recommendation

I reviewed four plant-care apps and compared how they handled symptoms, care history, and next steps.

I found that the existing products were strong at identification, reminders, or diagnosis, but they rarely helped users understand whether another change was actually necessary.

01

Immediate advice can create anxiety

Users may assume something must change right away.

02

History changes what is reasonable

Recent care actions affect what should happen next.

03

Waiting can be a valid action

Observation becomes easier when the product explains why.

03 · User

Maya needs reassurance before she needs another instruction

Maya is a new plant owner who becomes anxious when her plant looks different after being purchased or moved.

Primary persona

Goals: Understand what is happening, avoid making the problem worse, and know when to act.

Frustrations: Conflicting advice and recommendations that ignore what she already tried.

Needs: Reassurance, care history, one clear next step, and permission to wait.

Problem statement

Maya needs guidance that considers her plant's recent care history because generic instructions can make her change things too quickly and increase her anxiety.

04 · Design direction

One concern, one history, one next step

I focused on one feature instead of designing the entire app. The flow connects a plant's current concern with its recent care history.

01Plant concern
02Recent care history
03Current symptoms
04One recommendation
05Observe and check in
Possible recommendation Wait and check in again later

If the plant improves, continue the current care. If it does not, try one different action.

Paper wireframes

Exploring how the feature can slow the user down

I explored different ways to help Maya understand the diagnosis process, review recent care, and receive one clear recommendation.

Key design decision

Before recommending another change, Planted shows the user's recent care history. This gives the recommendation a reason and makes waiting feel like an informed decision.

05 · Next steps

The next question is whether users trust the pause

The project is currently at the paper-wireframe stage. I would test the feature before creating final visual design.

01

Build digital wireframes

Translate the paper explorations into a testable mobile flow.

02

Test the recommendation

Learn whether users understand why the feature recommends waiting.

03

Create a calm, readable, and accessible interface based on what users need.

Reflection

Helpful UX is not always about giving users more instructions. Sometimes it is about giving them enough context to feel confident waiting.

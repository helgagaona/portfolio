Project overview

The problem

Most plant-care apps give users immediate instructions based on a symptom without considering what they have already tried. Beginners may respond by repeatedly changing the plant's water, soil, light, or location, creating more stress instead of solving the problem.

Design goal

Help beginners understand what has already happened, then decide whether to make one change, continue observing, or wait during the plant's adaptation period.

My role

Solo UX/UI designer and researcher.

Responsibilities

Competitive research, personas, problem definition, feature structure, user flow, and paper wireframes.

Project scope

One focused feature rather than a complete plant-care app.

Research summary

Existing apps answer quickly, but rarely explain the context

I reviewed existing plant-care apps to understand how they respond to symptoms and care tasks. I focused on whether recommendations considered previous user actions or simply told users what to do immediately.

User research: pain points

Four opportunities shaped the feature

01

Immediate advice creates anxiety

Users may assume they need to change something right away when the plant may need time to adapt.

02

Care history is easy to forget

Recent watering, soil, light, and location changes can affect what is reasonable to do next.

03

Symptoms feel alarming

New plant owners need context that helps them understand whether a change is urgent or expected.

04

Waiting needs explanation

A helpful product should make observing the plant feel like an informed care decision.

Persona

Maya needs reassurance before she needs another instruction

Maya is the primary persona because her anxiety represents the central problem this feature is designed to solve.

Maya

Primary

A new plant owner who becomes anxious when her plant looks different after being purchased or moved.

Goals
Understand what is happening, avoid making the problem worse, and know when to act.
Frustrations
Conflicting advice and recommendations that ignore what she already tried.
Needs
Reassurance, care history, one clear next step, and permission to wait.

Problem statement

Maya needs guidance that considers her plant's recent care history because generic instructions can make her change things too quickly and increase her anxiety.

Feature structure

One concern, one history, one next step

The feature connects a plant's current concern with its recent care history. The larger app could include profiles, reminders, and progress tracking, but those areas remain outside this project's scope.

Paper wireframes

Exploring how the feature can slow the user down

I explored different ways to help Maya understand the diagnosis process, review recent care, and receive one clear recommendation.

01

Show context before advice

The care history gives the recommendation a reason instead of making it feel generic.

02

Give one reasonable action

Reducing competing instructions can help a beginner feel more confident.

03

Make waiting actionable

A check-in date gives the user a clear way to continue caring without overcorrecting.

Takeaway

Context can be more helpful than more instructions

Before recommending another change, Planted considers what has already happened to the plant.

What I learned

Waiting can be an active care decision

Good guidance does not always add another task. Sometimes it gives the user enough context to pause with confidence.

Helping new plant owners make calmer care decisions

I designed a context-aware plant-care feature for Planted that considers a plant’s recent care history before recommending what to do next—even when the best recommendation is to wait.

The problem

Most plant-care apps give users immediate instructions based on a symptom without considering what they have already tried. This can lead beginners to repeatedly change the plant’s water, soil, sunlight, or location, creating more stress instead of solving the problem.

My goal

Design one focused feature that records a plant’s recent care history and helps beginners decide whether to make one change, continue observing, or wait during the plant’s adaptation period.

Constraints

  • Solo UX/UI design project
  • Mobile-first feature
  • Designed for new plant owners with limited plant-care knowledge
  • One feature rather than a complete app
  • Current stage: paper wireframes

How I would know it worked

  • Users understand what has already happened to their plant.
  • Users know whether to act now or wait and observe.
  • Users receive one clear next step instead of several conflicting actions.
  • Users feel less anxious and less likely to overcorrect.

I used a user-centered design process to understand why beginners make repeated changes, identify gaps in existing plant-care apps, define the primary user, and design one focused feature before expanding the larger app concept.

Research

I reviewed existing plant-care apps to understand how they respond to plant symptoms and care tasks. I focused on whether their recommendations considered the user’s previous actions or simply told them what to do immediately.

What I learned

Immediate advice can create anxiety

Users may assume they need to change something right away when the plant may need time to adapt.

History changes the recommendation

Recent watering, soil, sunlight, and location changes can affect what is reasonable to do next.

Waiting can be a valid action

A helpful product should sometimes tell users to pause, observe, and check in again later.

Who I designed for

I focused on two users: beginners who need reassurance and experienced plant owners who want organized care history. Maya is the primary persona because her anxiety represents the main problem this feature is designed to solve.

Maya Primary

Profile: A new plant owner who becomes anxious when her plant looks different after being purchased or moved.

Goals: Understand what is happening, avoid making the problem worse, and know when to act.

Frustrations: Conflicting advice, unfamiliar symptoms, and recommendations that ignore what she already tried.

Sofia Secondary

Profile: An experienced plant rescuer who cares for plants in different locations and wants to understand how each plant responds over time.

Problem statement

Maya needs guidance that considers her plant’s recent care history because generic instructions can make her change the water, soil, light, or location too quickly and increase her anxiety.

How might we…

  • Help beginners understand what has already happened to their plant?
  • Help users decide whether to act, wait, or observe?
  • Give one reasonable next step instead of several immediate changes?
  • Make waiting feel like an informed care decision?

Feature flow and wireframes

Rather than designing the entire app, I focused on one feature: helping a new plant owner decide what to do next using the plant’s recent care history.

Feature structure

The feature connects a plant’s current concern with its recent care history. The larger app could include plant profiles, reminders, and progress tracking, but those areas are outside this project’s scope.

  • Plant concern
  • Recent care history
  • Current symptoms
  • Recommended next step
  • Observation period
  • Follow-up check-in

Primary user flow

Open a plant concern → Review recent care history → Describe the current symptom → Receive one recommended next step → Act or wait and observe → Return for a check-in → If the plant improves, continue the current care; if not, try one different action.

Key design decision

Most plant-care apps provide immediate advice based on a symptom. I chose to show the plant’s recent care history first so the recommendation could account for what the user has already done. The feature may recommend waiting, because observing the plant can be more reasonable than making another immediate change.

Immediate advice

Recommend watering, repotting, or moving the plant as soon as a symptom appears.

Context-aware guidance

Review recent actions first, then recommend one action, continued observation, or a waiting period.

Reflection

This project helped me understand that helpful UX is not always about giving users more instructions. Sometimes it is about giving them enough context to feel confident waiting.

What I learned

Plant-care guidance should consider the user’s previous actions. A recommendation to wait can be just as useful as a recommendation to water, repot, or move a plant.

What I would do differently

I would test the feature earlier with paper wireframes to learn whether users understand the care history and trust a recommendation to pause.
